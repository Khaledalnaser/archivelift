LinkedIn draft · based on “Social Media Manager vs Strategist vs Agency”

Delegating your posts is not the same as delegating your marketing.

You can hand off Canva. You can hand off scheduling. You can even hand off captions.

But if every campaign still waits for you to decide what matters, what the message is, what gets promoted, and whether the numbers mean anything, the mental load never actually left.

The question I’d ask before hiring another person to “help with marketing” is simple:

Who will own the decisions?

A social media manager can execute a channel. A strategist can shape the direction. A marketing director connects the decisions across the business and keeps the team moving from the same map.

If the answer to “who owns the marketing decisions?” is still “me,” that is usually the real bottleneck.

Suggested CTA: point to The Corner Office service guide or consult call, depending on the campaign.

Email draft · based on “Do You Actually Have the Capacity for Your Marketing Plan?”

Subject: Maybe the marketing plan isn’t the problem

A plan can make perfect sense on paper and still collapse in real life.

Not because you picked the wrong tactic. Not because you lack discipline. Sometimes the plan simply assumes more time, energy and decision-making capacity than your business actually has.

That matters because capacity is not an execution detail. It is a strategic input.

If you are the person holding the goals, the campaign calendar, the content decisions, the approvals and the measurement in your head, adding another content task does not create consistency. It adds another decision.

The better question is: what can this business sustain — and who needs to own what so marketing keeps moving without depending on you?

Suggested CTA: invite the reader to explore how the Cornerstone Strategy maps goals, channels and capacity before execution begins.
